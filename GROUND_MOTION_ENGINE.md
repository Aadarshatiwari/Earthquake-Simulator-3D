# Ground-motion engine

The earthquake lab supports sine, multifrequency, broadband, impulsive, long-duration, near-fault-inspired and seeded stochastic inputs, plus sourced CSV. Synthetic input uses an explicit duration, sample interval, carrier frequency, configurable spread where applicable, and a flat, tapered, or decaying envelope. The broadband and stochastic modes use deterministic seeded multisine components. The near-fault-inspired mode is a localized pulse, with no link to a particular fault or event.

For supported synthetic inputs, let V and D be the terminal velocity and displacement from two trapezoidal integrations from rest. Evaluate the same integrator on an all-ones vector and a unit normalized ramp. Subtract the corresponding affine acceleration correction so both terminal constraints vanish, then normalize the corrected samples to the selected PGA. The resulting acceleration is the actual solver input and the source of the displayed ground velocity and displacement. A flat sinusoid remains harmonic and omits ground translation. A corrected, finite pulse can have endpoint acceleration offsets. Correction here is a demonstration assumption, not a general strong-motion record processing method.

CSV from a user-specified source must have a uniform interval and header `time_s,accel_g` or `time_s,accel_m_s2`. One standard g is 9.80665 m/s². The lab retains provenance and feeds imported acceleration values to the solver in SI without baseline processing. Ground velocity and displacement are hidden for these recordings. Date, catalog magnitude and epicentre do not determine local acceleration. Frequency controls generate educational shapes; bandwidth entries are nominal frequency controls rather than a measured seismic spectrum.
